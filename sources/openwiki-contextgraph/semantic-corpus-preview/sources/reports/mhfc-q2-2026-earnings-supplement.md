# **Earnings Release** 

**CHARLOTTE BAY, July 14, 2026** - Meridian Harbor Financial Corp. today reported net income for the second quarter of 2026 of $6.54 billion, or $4.48 per diluted share, compared with net income of $6.12 billion, or $4.09 per share, in the second quarter of 2025. Reported revenue was $22.48 billion, up 5.4% year-on-year. 

##### **Second-quarter highlights** 

- Net income $6.54 billion; EPS $4.48; ROTCE 25% 

- Revenue $22.48 billion, up 5.4% YoY; NII $12.56 billion, up 4.5% 

- Average loans up 6% YoY; average deposits up 4% YoY 

- Credit costs $1.92 billion: net charge-offs $1.65 billion, net reserve build $270 million 

- Standardized CET1 ratio 15.1%; returned $4.6 billion to shareholders 

_"Our businesses performed well this quarter. Clients are active, credit is behaving as we expected and our balance sheet has never been stronger. We remain alert to a wide range of economic outcomes and continue to manage the company to be ready for all of them." - Eleanor V. Ashcombe, Chairman and CEO_ 

### **Firmwide results** 

Net interest income was $12.56 billion, up 2.0% from the prior quarter, reflecting one additional day, Card loan growth and higher deposit balances, partly offset by lower deposit margins following the 25 basis point policy rate reduction in March. Noninterest revenue was $9.92 billion, down 3.1% sequentially from a seasonally strong first quarter in Markets, and up 6.6% year-on-year on higher investment banking and asset management fees. 

Noninterest expense was $12.14 billion, up 3.3% year-on-year, driven by compensation, including growth in front-office and technology headcount, and continued technology investment, partly offset by lower legal expense. The overhead ratio was 54.0%. 

The provision for credit losses was $1.92 billion, reflecting net charge-offs of $1.65 billion and a net reserve build of $270 million, primarily in Card on loan growth. The allowance for loan losses was $16.38 billion, or 2.15% of period-end loans. 

### **Outlook** 

Management now expects full-year 2026 net interest income of approximately $50.5 billion, up from the approximately $50.2 billion expectation provided in January, reflecting stronger deposit balances. Adjusted noninterest expense is expected to be approximately $49.5 billion, and the Card net charge-off rate is expected to be approximately 3.6%. The outlook assumes the market-implied forward rate curve as of June 30, 2026.

**Management commentary.** Consumer spending remained resilient across income bands, with discretionary categories such as travel and dining growing faster than non-discretionary spend. ### **Earnings Call Highlights - Analyst Q&A** 

Summary of selected questions and management responses from the July 14, 2026 conference call. Responses are paraphrased for brevity. 

#### **Deposit trends** 

**Analyst:** Can you talk about deposit pricing and how much further deposit margins can compress if the Fed cuts twice more? 

**Rajiv N. Castellano:** We have assumed two more 25 basis point cuts in our outlook. Our models assume betas of about 45% on consumer interest-bearing and 75% on wholesale interest-bearing balances on the way down, and so far realized betas are tracking those assumptions. The bigger swing factor is mix: if customers keep moving from checking to savings and CDs, that costs us more than the rate cuts themselves. 

#### **NII sensitivity** 

**Analyst:** You remain asset-sensitive. Why not add more duration to protect NII in a down-rate scenario? 

**Helen T. Mbeki:** We have been adding duration, which is why we took securities losses last year. A 200 basis point decline would reduce NII by roughly 6% on a static basis, which is inside our 7% Board limit. We would rather keep flexibility than lock in today's yields, and our dynamic simulations, which include management actions, show a smaller impact. 

#### **Card credit** 

**Analyst:** Card charge-offs are at 3.58%. Are you comfortable with the reserve, and what would change it? 

**Dana K. Whitfield:** Yes. We hold a Card allowance of roughly 6% of loans, which covers almost two years of current charge-offs. The allowance is driven mainly by loan growth and by our scenario weights. If we moved entirely to the downside scenario, the Firmwide allowance would rise by over $4 billion, so the weighting matters more than small changes in the baseline. 

#### **Capital deployment** 

**Analyst:** You have over $20 billion of excess capital above your target by 2028. Why not buy back more? 

**Rajiv N. Castellano:** Our capital model shows the excess building, but we are deliberately cautious. There is still uncertainty about the final capital rules, and we would rather deploy capital into client growth. We will revisit the buyback pace after the final SCB is confirmed in August. 

#### **Office CRE** 

**Analyst:** How much more pain is left in office real estate? 

**Dana K. Whitfield:** Criticized office loans are down for the second quarter in a row, and our allowance coverage on office is close to 10%. We expect losses to remain elevated but manageable; office is about 2% of total loans. 

#### **Technology and APIs** 

**Analyst:** What is the payoff from the API platform beyond client products? 

**Marcus O. Lindqvist:** The same governed APIs we sell to clients feed our risk models. For example, moving the Macroeconomic Scenario API to our strategic platform cut the scenario load for the CECL and capital models from six hours to forty minutes, which lets us run more scenarios and close the quarter faster with better controls.
