# Consumer & Community Banking

**Consumer & Community Banking (CCB)** is the retail and small-business segment of [Meridian Harbor Financial Corp.](meridian-harbor-financial-corp.md), a fictional institution described in a synthetic proof-of-concept document set. It is the largest of the three reportable business segments, alongside Commercial & Investment Bank (CIB) and [Asset & Wealth Management](asset-wealth-management.md) (AWM). A fourth "Corporate" segment holds Treasury/CIO and unallocated central costs. These segments are the Firm's organizing structure ([Annual Report, MD&A Executive Overview, p. 7](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-annual-report.md#L98)).

All figures below come from three documents: the 2025 Annual Report (FY2025, published February 2026), the 2025 Pillar 3 Disclosures (March 2026) and the Q2 2026 Earnings Supplement (released 14 July 2026). Amounts are in U.S. dollars. Segment results are shown on a managed basis ([Annual Report, Business Segment Results, p. 9](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-annual-report.md#L166-L168)).

## What CCB is and does

CCB offers deposit, investment and lending products, payments and services to consumers and small businesses. It does so through four lines of business: **Banking & Wealth Management, Home Lending, Card Services and Auto**. At FY2025 it served 74 million customers through 4,180 branches, 15,900 ATMs and digital channels ([Annual Report, p. 9](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-annual-report.md#L180)).

The segment is where most consumer credit risk and most consumer funding sit. Credit Card, Residential Mortgage and Auto are the consumer loan classes. Consumer loans totaled $445.9 billion at year-end 2025. The Firm's loans were $742.3 billion ([Annual Report, Consumer credit portfolio, p. 16](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-annual-report.md#L355-L357)). About 66% of Firm deposits come from consumer and small-business clients ([Annual Report, Liquidity Risk Management, p. 15](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-annual-report.md#L331)).

The Annual Report gives CCB its own capital and return framework. Capital is allocated to each segment on its standalone risk profile. That profile includes the Standardized-approach requirement, the stress capital buffer and the G-SIB surcharge. Segment ROE is net income less allocated preferred dividends, divided by average allocated equity ([p. 9](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-annual-report.md#L168)).

## Card credit and the main risk drivers

Card is both CCB's growth engine and its main source of credit cost.

| Card metric | 2024 | 2025 | 2Q25 | 1Q26 | 2Q26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NCO rate | 3.18% | 3.42% | 3.39% | 3.55% | 3.58% |
| 30+ day delinquency | 2.19% (implied) | 2.31% | 2.15% | 2.29% | 2.24% |
| 90+ day delinquency | n/a | 1.18% | 1.09% | 1.19% | 1.14% |

Sources: [Annual Report p. 9](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-annual-report.md#L184) and [p. 16](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-annual-report.md#L357); [Q2 2026 Consumer Credit Quality, p. 14](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-q2-2026-earnings-supplement.md#L340-L349). The 2.19% figure is derived from the report's statement that FY2025 30+ day delinquency of 2.31% was "up 12 basis points". It is not printed directly.

Key facts:

- **Provision composition.** FY2025 segment provision of $5.96 billion reflected Card net charge-offs of $4.6 billion and a $600 million reserve build. Home Lending net charge-offs were only $40 million ([Annual Report p. 9](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-annual-report.md#L184)). The Card line of the allowance roll-forward matches: $8,040 million opening balance, less $4,620 million NCOs, plus $5,220 million provision, gives $8,640 million ([Note 6, p. 26](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-annual-report.md#L598-L606)).
- **Card dominates consumer charge-offs.** In 2Q26 Card NCOs were $1,270 million, Auto $100 million and Residential Mortgage $10 million. Card was about 77% of total Firm NCOs of $1,650 million. Card NCOs were up 14.4% year on year ([Q2 2026 Credit Trends, p. 11](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-q2-2026-earnings-supplement.md#L254-L262)). See [Net Charge-offs](../metrics/net-charge-offs.md) for the full series and for the 3.42% versus 3.34% Card-rate difference. The 3.34% figure appears in the credit-risk table. The 3.42% figure appears in the CCB discussion and the risk appetite table.
- **Reserve coverage.** The Card allowance was $8,640 million, or 6.24% of Card loans, at year-end 2025. That is $8,383 million modeled plus a $257 million qualitative overlay ([Annual Report p. 17](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-annual-report.md#L377-L385)). The Chief Risk Officer said on the Q2 2026 call that this covers almost two years of current charge-offs. She said the allowance is driven mainly by loan growth and by scenario weights ([Q2 2026 Q&A, p. 18](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-q2-2026-earnings-supplement.md#L446-L450)). The wider mechanics are in [Allowance for Credit Losses](../metrics/allowance-for-credit-losses.md).
- **Other consumer classes.** Residential mortgage credit is very strong: a 0.02% NCO rate, weighted-average current LTV of 51% (50% by 2Q26) and FICO of 768. Auto delinquencies were stable at year-end. The 2Q26 auto NCO rate was 0.61% ([Annual Report p. 16](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-annual-report.md#L357); [Q2 2026 p. 14](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-q2-2026-earnings-supplement.md#L340-L348)).
- **Vintage performance.** New Card loan growth reflects strong account origination in the second half of 2025 maturing into revolving balances. Management reports that these vintages are performing in line with underwriting expectations. Early-stage roll rates track the underwriting loss curves ([Q2 2026 p. 7](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-q2-2026-earnings-supplement.md#L169); [p. 14](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-q2-2026-earnings-supplement.md#L351)).

### Guidance and limits

Management expects the full-year 2026 Card NCO rate to be about 3.6%. This was set in the Annual Report outlook and reiterated unchanged in the Q2 2026 release ([Annual Report p. 7](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-annual-report.md#L116); [Q2 2026 p. 3](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-q2-2026-earnings-supplement.md#L51)). The Board's risk appetite sets an annual Card NCO ceiling of 4.5%. The FY2025 rate of 3.42% was reported as "Within" that limit ([Pillar 3 §2 Risk appetite, p. 5](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-pillar3-disclosures.md#L66-L77)). These outlook statements are forward-looking and carry the usual caveats.

## Credit decisioning, fraud and data dependencies

CCB's credit and servicing operations rely on shared firmwide services published on the Firm's Developer Platform:

- **Origination.** Consumer credit decisions use automated scorecards exposed through the **Credit Decisioning API (API-09)**. These combine bureau data, internal behavior scores and fraud signals from the **Fraud Risk Signals API (API-08)** ([Annual Report p. 15](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-annual-report.md#L341); [Pillar 3 §6, p. 10](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-pillar3-disclosures.md#L216)). In 2Q26 a sandbox of the Credit Decisioning API was launched for point-of-sale lending partners ([Q2 2026 p. 17](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-q2-2026-earnings-supplement.md#L426)).
- **Reserving.** Expected lifetime losses on new Card balances are reserved through the CECL Lifetime Expected Credit Loss Model (MDL-CR-007) as loans are booked ([Q2 2026 p. 7](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-q2-2026-earnings-supplement.md#L169)). Pool-level PD and LGD come from the **Credit Risk Scoring API (API-10)**, refreshed in June 2026 and reflected in the quarter-end allowance. The model sources balances and remaining lives from the Loan Servicing API (API-11) and scenarios from the Macroeconomic Scenario API (API-14). Its owner is Consumer & Wholesale Credit Risk – Allowance Methodology ([Annual Report p. 16](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-annual-report.md#L365-L367); [Q2 2026 p. 14](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-q2-2026-earnings-supplement.md#L351)).
- **Customer channels.** Account and transaction data in the consumer apps are served through the Accounts API and Transactions API, which are also offered to aggregators under data-access agreements ([Annual Report p. 9](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-annual-report.md#L200)). In 2Q26 these two APIs handled 1,140 million and 1,320 million calls per month, with growth of 18% and 22% year on year. The Card Management API handled 260 million calls per month ([Q2 2026 Technology and Digital Update, p. 17](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-q2-2026-earnings-supplement.md#L413-L421)).
- **Fraud.** Real-time scoring through API-08 applies to every card authorization, real-time payment and wire. Firm-wide fraud losses were 7.9 basis points of card sales in 2025 ([Annual Report, Operational Risk, p. 20-21](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-annual-report.md#L465)).
- **Payments.** Customers initiated 4.1 billion Zelle-style person-to-person and real-time payments in 2025 ([Annual Report p. 9](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-annual-report.md#L200)).

Because these APIs also feed Tier 1 models and regulatory reporting, a schema or business-logic change in an upstream API triggers a model change review ([Annual Report, Model Risk Management, p. 20](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-annual-report.md#L439)). That is a relevant constraint when changing CCB origination or servicing data.

## How CCB connects to the rest of the Firm

- **Funding and liquidity.** CCB deposits are the Firm's primary funding base. Average CCB deposits were $612 billion in 2025 and $628 billion in 2Q26. Firmwide consumer deposits were $668.9 billion at 2Q26 period end ([Q2 2026 p. 13](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-q2-2026-earnings-supplement.md#L332)). The segment average and the Firm consumer-deposit total are on different bases, so they should not be compared directly. Retail and small-business deposit outflows are the first line of the LCR calculation, with $41.8 billion weighted outflows against $668.9 billion unweighted ([Q2 2026 LCR detail, p. 15](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-q2-2026-earnings-supplement.md#L379)). See [Liquidity Coverage Ratio](../metrics/liquidity-coverage-ratio.md).
- **Net interest income.** CCB generated $31.4 billion of the Firm's $48.6 billion NII in 2025. For 2026, the Firm's NII outlook cites Card loan growth (about +$1.1 billion) as the largest positive driver. Lower policy rates on deposit margins (about -$1.0 billion) and migration to higher-yielding deposit products (about -$0.4 billion) offset it ([Q2 2026 p. 15-16](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-q2-2026-earnings-supplement.md#L401)). The NII Sensitivity Model assumes a 0.45 beta on consumer interest-bearing deposits. The realized cumulative interest-bearing deposit beta of 49% is slightly above this and is a blend with wholesale deposits ([Q2 2026 p. 13](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-q2-2026-earnings-supplement.md#L319)). Management (Rajiv N. Castellano) said product mix, meaning movement from checking to savings and CDs, is a bigger swing factor than rate cuts ([Q2 2026 Q&A, p. 18](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-q2-2026-earnings-supplement.md#L438)). See [Net Interest Income](../metrics/net-interest-income.md).
- **Capital.** Retail credit risk RWA was $196.8 billion, or 30.1% of Standardized RWA of $652.8 billion. Card drove +$9.8 billion of the credit-risk RWA growth in 2025 ([Pillar 3 §4.1, p. 7-8](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-pillar3-disclosures.md#L135-L168)). Advanced RWA grew less than Standardized because internal PD and LGD estimates for Card reflect the high credit quality of new originations ([Pillar 3 p. 7](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-pillar3-disclosures.md#L151)). See [Risk-Weighted Assets](../metrics/risk-weighted-assets.md) and [CET1 Ratio](../metrics/cet1-ratio.md).
- **Securitization.** The Firm originates through a credit card master trust, with $8.2 billion of retained interests and $1.64 billion of RWA ([Pillar 3 §8, p. 13](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-pillar3-disclosures.md#L331-L340)). The source describes this as Firm-level activity and does not attribute it to CCB by name. Card is the obvious origin, but that is an inference.

## Reporting conventions and caveats

- **Card NCO rate definitions.** The quarterly Card NCO rate is annualized Card NCOs divided by average Card loans ([Q2 2026 glossary, p. 21](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-q2-2026-earnings-supplement.md#L528)). The FY2025 credit-risk table shows 3.34% (4,620 ÷ 138,400 period-end loans), while the CCB discussion shows 3.42%. The sources do not explain the gap, though it is consistent with a denominator difference.
- **Provision wording.** The CCB text states "$6.0 billion" and the table shows 5,960. Both refer to the same figure.
- **Nonaccrual policy.** Consumer loans are generally placed on nonaccrual at 90 days past due. Credit cards accrue until charged off at 180 days. This is why the Card nonaccrual balance is shown as "-" in loan tables ([Annual Report Note 2, p. 25](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-annual-report.md#L574); [Note 5, p. 25](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-annual-report.md#L584)). Card 90+ days past due was $1,633 million ([Pillar 3 §6.4, p. 12](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-pillar3-disclosures.md#L289)).
- **Firmwide line items.** Firmwide Card income (a noninterest revenue line) fell 20.0% in 2025 to $1,160 million and fell 21.4% year on year in 2Q26, even as Card net interest income grew ([Annual Report p. 8](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-annual-report.md#L141); [Q2 2026 p. 6](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-q2-2026-earnings-supplement.md#L124)). The sources do not break this line out by segment.
- **Synthetic data.** Every document states that Meridian Harbor is fictional and the figures are invented ([Annual Report cover, p. 1](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-2025-annual-report.md#L15)).

## Outlook signals

The Firm-level 2026 outlook is Q2-updated net interest income of about $50.5 billion (up from about $50.2 billion), adjusted noninterest expense of about $49.5 billion and a Card NCO rate of about 3.6%. CCB-specific drivers noted are Card loan growth, deposit mix and margin compression as policy rates fall ([Q2 2026 Outlook, p. 3](repo://sources/reports/mhfc-q2-2026-earnings-supplement.md#L49-L51)). No segment-level 2026 targets are published.
