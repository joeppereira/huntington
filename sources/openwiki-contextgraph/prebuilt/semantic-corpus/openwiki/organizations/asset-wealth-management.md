# Asset & Wealth Management (AWM)

## Description

AWM is one of the three reportable business segments of [Meridian Harbor Financial Corp.](meridian-harbor-financial-corp.md) (MHFC), alongside [Consumer & Community Banking](consumer-community-banking.md) (CCB) and [Commercial & Investment Bank](commercial-investment-bank.md) (CIB). The [Corporate](corporate.md) segment (Treasury/CIO and Other Corporate) is reported separately (Annual Report 2025, MD&A "Executive Overview", p. 7).

AWM "offers investment and wealth management solutions to institutions, retail investors and high-net-worth individuals across equities, fixed income, alternatives, multi-asset and liquidity strategies, as well as lending, banking and trust services through Global Private Bank" (Annual Report 2025, "Business Segment Results", p. 10).

## Place in the bank

Segment results are presented on a managed basis. Capital is allocated to each segment on its standalone risk profile, including Standardized-approach regulatory capital requirements, the stress capital buffer and the G-SIB surcharge. Segment ROE is net income less allocated preferred dividends, divided by average allocated equity (Annual Report 2025, p. 9).

FY2025 segment comparison (Annual Report 2025, "Segment results", p. 9; $ in millions):

| Segment | Net revenue 2025 | Net income 2025 | ROE 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| CCB | 40,800 | 12,300 | 30% |
| CIB | 32,450 | 10,180 | 18% |
| **AWM** | **13,180** | **3,580** | **32%** |
| Corporate | (330) | (1,100) | NM |
| Total Firm | 86,100 | 24,960 | 21% |

AWM is the smallest of the three business segments by revenue and net income, yet has the highest ROE (32% versus Total Firm 21%). It contributed $3,580 million of the firm's $24,960 million net income (see [Net Income and EPS](../metrics/net-income-and-eps.md)).

Firmwide asset management fees were $9,120 million in 2025 versus $8,340 million in 2024 (+9.4%). The annual report attributes the rise to higher average market levels and strong net inflows (Annual Report 2025, "Net revenue" and noninterest revenue table, pp. 7-8). The same report cites higher revenue-related compensation in CIB and AWM as a driver of the 4.8% increase in firmwide compensation expense (p. 8).

Firmwide net income for 2Q26 was $6,540 million (-0.3% QoQ, +6.9% YoY), so AWM's 2Q26 net income of $960 million rose faster than the firm's on both comparisons (p. 4).

Additional 2Q26 disclosures (p. 9):

- Asset management fees of $2.48 billion, up 10% YoY on higher average market levels and net inflows.
- Long-term net inflows of $41 billion and liquidity net inflows of $18 billion.
- AUM of $2.27 trillion and client assets of $3.31 trillion, both records.
- Pre-tax margin of 36% and ROE of 34% (versus 32.0% for FY2025).

Management commentary (p. 9): record client assets reflect market appreciation and continued net new money, particularly in Global Private Bank and active ETFs, where the firm ranks among the top five issuers by net flows; 84% of ten-year long-term mutual fund AUM performed above the peer median. Advisor headcount rose 5% YoY. Lending to private bank clients, primarily securities-based lending and mortgages, grew 7% with negligible credit losses. Alternatives fundraising raised $9 billion across private credit, infrastructure and real estate strategies in the quarter.

## Credit and capital notes

The 2025 Pillar 3 disclosures say the improvement in asset quality and collateral within credit risk RWA "primarily reflects higher eligible collateral on securities-based lending in Asset & Wealth Management" (Pillar 3 Disclosures 2025, section 4.1a, p. 8). The change-in-asset-quality line in that RWA flow was $(1,400) million.
