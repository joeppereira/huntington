# Net Income and EPS

The figures come from two reports: the [2025 Annual Report](../reports/annual-report-2025.md) for full-year 2025, and the [Q2 2026 Earnings Supplement](../reports/q2-2026-earnings-supplement.md) for the quarter ended June 30, 2026. The sections below give the reported values, how they are built up, and what moved them. Dollar figures are in millions unless stated otherwise.

## How EPS is derived

EPS is not net income divided by shares. Preferred dividends come off first:

1. Net income is pre-tax income less income tax expense.
2. Preferred dividends are subtracted to give net income applicable to common stockholders.
3. That amount is divided by average diluted shares.

Checking the reported numbers:

- 2Q26 has 6,540 of net income and 260 of preferred dividends. That leaves 6,280 for common stockholders, which over 1,402 mm shares is about $4.48.
- FY2025 has 24,960 of net income. Net income applicable to common is 23,900, a difference of about $1.06 billion, which Pillar 3 rounds to $1.1 billion of preferred dividends. Divided by 1,412 mm shares this is $16.93.
- 2Q25 had 270 of preferred dividends, which gives 5,850 applicable to common.

The 2Q26 supplement's income statement shows a preferred dividends line of 260, and a separate reconciliation shows net income applicable to common of 6,280. The preferred-dividend step is therefore consistent across the documents.

### Year-on-year change (2Q26 vs 2Q25)

Net income rose $420 million. Revenue added $1,150 million: net interest income +$540 million and noninterest revenue +$610 million. Expense (+$390 million) and provision (+$280 million) absorbed part of that, leaving pre-tax income up $480 million. Higher tax (+$60 million) left net income up $420 million.

- **Net interest income** rose 4.5% year-on-year on Card loan growth and higher deposit balances. Lower deposit margins following the 25 bp March policy rate cut partly offset these.
- **Noninterest revenue** rose 6.6% year-on-year on investment banking fees (+13.8%) and asset management fees (+10.2%).
- **Noninterest expense** rose 3.3% year-on-year, driven by compensation (including front-office and technology headcount) and technology investment, partly offset by lower legal expense.
- **Provision for credit losses** rose 17.1% year-on-year. It comprised $1.65 billion of net charge-offs and a $270 million net reserve build, mainly in Card on loan growth.
- **Share count.** Average diluted shares fell 2.0% year-on-year. This is why EPS grew 9.5% against 6.9% for net income. A $3.0 billion buyback in the quarter continues the reduction.

### Sequential change (2Q26 vs 1Q26)

Net income was essentially flat, down $20 million. Net interest income gained +$250 million, helped by one more day and Card growth. Noninterest revenue fell $320 million from a seasonally strong Markets quarter, with principal transactions down 11.9%. Expense fell $250 million, led by compensation (-3.7%), and provision rose $140 million. Because average diluted shares fell 0.4% (1,407 mm to 1,402 mm) and preferred dividends were flat, EPS stayed at $4.48.

### Profitability measures built on net income

ROTCE is annualized net income applicable to common divided by average tangible common equity (average TCE of $103.0 billion in 2Q26). The supplement shows it inconsistently for 2Q26. The highlights say 25%, but the non-GAAP reconciliation and the five-quarter table both show 24%. The reconciliation inputs (6,280 × 4 ÷ 103,000) work out to about 24.4%, which supports the 24% figure. The 2Q25 figure also differs between the reconciliation (25%) and the trend table (24%). Treat ROTCE as a rounded, non-GAAP measure and cite the table you use.

## Outlook and sensitivities

The Q2 2026 release does not guide net income or EPS directly. It guides inputs to earnings:

- Full-year 2026 net interest income of about $50.5 billion, raised from $50.2 billion in January on stronger deposit balances.
- Adjusted noninterest expense of about $49.5 billion.
- A Card net charge-off rate of about 3.6%.

The outlook assumes the market-implied forward curve as of June 30, 2026. Management's commentary names the main swing factors:

- **Deposit mix.** Customers moving from checking to savings and CDs squeezes margin more than rate cuts do.
- **Allowance scenario weights.** Provision depends on the CECL scenario weights (Upside 20%, Baseline 50%, Downside 30%, unchanged from year end). Applying 100% to the downside scenario would raise the year-end 2025 allowance by about $4.2 billion, which shows why the weights matter more to provision than small baseline changes.
- **Rate sensitivity.** A 200 bp parallel decline in rates would lower 12-month NII by about 6%, within the 7% Board limit.
- **Buybacks.** Share repurchases of $3.0 billion per quarter reduce the denominator of EPS. Management said it would revisit the pace after the final stress capital buffer is confirmed.

## Caveats

- The source documents are labeled synthetic and describe a fictional institution.
- Quarterly figures are rounded, and the documents state that totals may not sum due to rounding.
- Regulatory capital ratios in the supplement are estimates pending the FR Y-9C filing. The income statement figures are presented as GAAP results.
- The sources do not name the Chief Financial Officer, and the earnings call shows a Q&A speaker (Rajiv N. Castellano) answering capital and deposit questions without a title. Do not infer the role from that.
