# Q2 2026 Earnings Release and Financial Supplement

Meridian Harbor Financial Corp. (MHFC) released its second-quarter 2026 earnings release and financial supplement on July 14, 2026, with a conference call at 8:30 a.m. ET (page 1). The document is explicitly a synthetic proof-of-concept: the company is fictional and all figures are invented (pages 1, 21). Page references below, such as "(p. 5)", point to the page markers in the converted source document.

Related metric pages: [Net income and EPS](../metrics/net-income-and-eps.md), [Net interest income](../metrics/net-interest-income.md), [Net charge-offs](../metrics/net-charge-offs.md), [Allowance for credit losses](../metrics/allowance-for-credit-losses.md), [CET1 ratio](../metrics/cet1-ratio.md), [Liquidity coverage ratio](../metrics/liquidity-coverage-ratio.md).

Net income was flat sequentially (-0.3%) and up 6.9% year-on-year. EPS rose 9.5% year-on-year, faster than net income, because average diluted shares fell from 1,431 million to 1,402 million (-2.0%) (p. 5). The effective tax rate was 22.3%, against 21.7% in 1Q26 and 22.9% in 2Q25 (p. 5). Pre-tax income of $8.42 billion less tax of $1.88 billion gives the $6.54 billion net income (p. 6).

## What drove net income

**Revenue (p. 3, p. 6).** Revenue grew 5.4% year-on-year but slipped 0.3% sequentially.
- NII rose 2.0% quarter on quarter, reflecting one additional day, Card loan growth and higher deposit balances. Lower deposit margins after the 25 bp policy-rate cut in March partly offset these.
- Noninterest revenue fell 3.1% sequentially from a seasonally strong Markets first quarter. It was up 6.6% year-on-year on investment banking fees (+13.8%) and asset management fees (+10.2%).
- Principal transactions fell 11.9% sequentially to $3.18 billion, and card income fell 21.4% year-on-year.

**Expense (p. 3, p. 6).** Noninterest expense of $12.14 billion was up 3.3% year-on-year, on compensation (front-office and technology headcount) and technology investment, partly offset by lower legal expense. It was down 2.0% sequentially, mainly on compensation (-3.7%) and other expense (-9.4%).

**Provision (p. 3, p. 5).** The provision rose 7.9% sequentially and 17.1% year-on-year to $1.92 billion. This was the largest increase of any major line, and it was driven by Card growth (see [Credit](#credit)).

Management says deposit growth came from customers moving cash from money market funds into savings and CDs, which carry a lower margin than checking balances (p. 7).
- **CIB:** Sequential weakness came from Markets ($4.61 billion, -9.3% quarter on quarter) after a record first-quarter derivatives performance. Investment banking fees were $1.82 billion (+13.8% year-on-year) and Payments revenue was $2.5 billion (+8%). Real-time payment volume rose 64% after the Real-Time Payments API was extended to middle-market clients (p. 8; see [API-04](../apis/api-04-real-time-payments-api.md)).
- **AWM:** Client assets reached a record $3.31 trillion and AUM $2.27 trillion. Long-term net inflows were $41 billion, with a 36% pre-tax margin and 34% ROE (p. 9).
- **Corporate:** The net loss of $370 million includes $40 million of securities losses and $480 million of unallocated technology and legal expense. Treasury/CIO NII benefited from reinvesting maturing securities at higher yields (p. 10).

## Net interest income

NII was $12.56 billion; the five-quarter path is 12,020 → 12,200 → 12,390 → 12,310 → 12,560 (p. 20). See [Net interest income](../metrics/net-interest-income.md) for the metric definition.

**Average balance sheet (p. 13).**
- Average interest-earning assets were $1,452.0 billion at a 5.48% yield.
- Interest-bearing liabilities were $991.2 billion at a 2.95% rate.
- The net interest yield was 3.47%.
- Loans yielded 7.04% on $756.9 billion.
- Interest-bearing deposits cost 2.48% on $758.2 billion.
- Loan yields fell 9 bp sequentially because floating-rate C&I and CRE loans repriced after the March cut, partly offset by mix shift toward Card.
- The cost of interest-bearing deposits fell 7 bp.

**Deposit betas.**
- The model assumes 45% (consumer) and 75% (wholesale), and these were re-calibrated in 1Q and left unchanged (p. 15).
- The realized cumulative interest-bearing deposit beta since the start of the easing cycle is 49% (p. 13).
- On the call, the CFO said realized betas were tracking assumptions, and that deposit mix (checking shifting to savings and CDs) is a bigger swing factor than the rate cuts themselves. The outlook assumes two further 25 bp cuts (p. 18).

**Rate sensitivity (p. 15, p. 18).**
- The Net Interest Income Sensitivity Model ([MDL-ALM-014](../models/nii-sensitivity-model.md)) applies instantaneous parallel shocks to a static balance sheet.
- The firm is modestly asset-sensitive. The table shown is the year-end 2025 disclosure: ±100 bp moves 12-month NII by ±$1,508 million (±3.0%), and ±200 bp moves it by ±$3,017 million (±6.0%).
- At June 30, 2026 sensitivity was described as "directionally similar", with +100 bp adding about $1.4 billion.
- Management said a 200 bp decline (about -6%) is inside a 7% Board limit, and that dynamic simulations including management actions show a smaller impact.

**2026 outlook drivers (p. 15).** Relative to 2025, the NII bridge to about $50.5 billion is:

| Driver | Effect |
|---|---|
| Card loan growth | about +$1.1 billion |
| Reinvestment of maturing securities at higher yields | +$0.6 billion |
| Deposit growth | +$0.5 billion |
| Lower policy rates on deposit margins | -$1.0 billion |
| Migration to higher-yielding deposit products | -$0.4 billion |
| Other balance sheet effects (not shown separately) | about +$1.1 billion |

## Credit

The overall message is that credit is consistent with management's expectations. Consumer credit is normalizing gradually, led by Card. Wholesale credit is stable, with losses concentrated in office CRE. Nonperforming assets were $5.4 billion, or 0.71% of loans (p. 11).

**Net charge-offs (p. 11).** Total net charge-offs were $1.65 billion, up 3.8% sequentially and 8.6% year-on-year. See [Net charge-offs](../metrics/net-charge-offs.md).

Card accounts for about 77% of the total. The firmwide net charge-off rate has moved from 0.84% (2Q25) to 0.88% (p. 20).

**Card (p. 7, p. 14).** The Card net charge-off rate was 3.58%, against 3.55% in 1Q26 and 3.39% in 2Q25. Management describes it as in line with guidance. The 30+ day delinquency rate improved seasonally to 2.24% from 2.29%, and the 90+ day rate to 1.14%. Early-stage roll rates for the 2025 vintages track underwriting loss curves. Card originations are decided through the [Credit Decisioning API](../apis/api-09-credit-decisioning-api.md). Pool-level PD and LGD estimates from the [Credit Risk Scoring API](../apis/api-10-credit-risk-scoring-api.md) were refreshed in June and are reflected in the quarter-end allowance.

**Office CRE (p. 8, p. 14, p. 18).**
- Office exposure is $15.2 billion, of which 38% is investment grade and $3.1 billion is criticized. Criticized balances were $3.6 billion at year-end.
- 2Q26 office net charge-offs were $90 million, which is the whole of the CRE line in the NCO table.
- Allowance coverage on the office portfolio is 9.8%, and office is about 2% of total loans.
- Management expects losses to remain "elevated but manageable".

**Allowance roll-forward (p. 11).** The allowance is the line that links the provision to the net charge-offs. See [Allowance for credit losses](../metrics/allowance-for-credit-losses.md).

The $270 million net reserve build is the provision minus net charge-offs, "primarily in Card on loan growth" (p. 3).

**CECL inputs (p. 11, p. 18).**
- The allowance uses the CECL Lifetime Expected Credit Loss Model ([MDL-CR-007](../models/cecl-allowance-model.md)).
- Scenario weights are unchanged from year-end: Upside 20%, Baseline 50%, Downside 30%.
- The baseline assumes unemployment peaking at 4.5% in 1Q27, up from the 4.4% assumed at year-end.
- The CRO said a Card allowance of roughly 6% of loans covers almost two years of current charge-offs. Moving fully to the downside scenario would raise the firmwide allowance by more than $4 billion, so scenario weighting matters more than small baseline changes.

## Capital and liquidity

**CET1 (pp. 4, 5, 12).** See [CET1 ratio](../metrics/cet1-ratio.md).
- The Standardized CET1 ratio was 15.1% (15.14% on $101.2 billion CET1 capital over $668.4 billion RWA), flat against 1Q26.
- CET1 capital grew 1.1% and RWA grew 1.1%, so the ratio did not move. The text attributes the flat ratio to net income being offset by distributions and RWA growth.
- The firm bought back $3.0 billion of stock and paid $1.15 per common share in dividends, about $4.6 billion returned in total.
- The supplementary leverage ratio was 6.1%.

**Requirement stack (p. 12).** The total CET1 requirement is 10.2%: 4.5% regulatory minimum, 3.2% SCB and 2.5% G-SIB surcharge, with a 0.0% countercyclical buffer. Management's own target is 13.0%.

**Stress capital buffer (p. 4, p. 12).** The Federal Reserve gave a preliminary 2026 SCB on June 27, 2026, and the firm expects it to stay at 3.2% from October 1, 2026. The firm's own indicative SCB of 3.18% is the severely adverse peak-to-trough CET1 decline (2.24%) plus 0.94% for dividends.

**Capital plan (p. 12).** The plan is produced with the Capital Planning & Stress Projection Model ([MDL-CAP-003](../models/capital-planning-model.md)) from June 30, 2026 starting capital.
- *Baseline:* CET1 rises to 16.15% by 3Q28 after $27.0 billion of repurchases ($3.0 billion a quarter) and $1.15 quarterly dividends per share.
- *Severely adverse:* the minimum CET1 is 12.90% with buybacks suspended.
- *Excess capital:* about $23.0 billion above the 13.0% target by 3Q28.
- On the call, management said it is being cautious because of uncertainty about final capital rules, would rather deploy capital into client growth, and will revisit the buyback pace after the final SCB is confirmed in August (p. 18).

**Liquidity (pp. 4, 5, 15).** See [Liquidity coverage ratio](../metrics/liquidity-coverage-ratio.md).
- The average LCR was 115%, down from 117%.
- HQLA was $292.3 billion against net cash outflows of $254.2 billion.
- Total liquidity resources were about $560 billion.
- HQLA and cash-flow projections are compiled daily from the Treasury Liquidity Positions API (API-15).

## Outlook (p. 3)

- Full-year 2026 NII of about $50.5 billion, raised from about $50.2 billion in January because of stronger deposit balances.
- Adjusted noninterest expense of about $49.5 billion. Adjusted expense excludes legal expense and the FDIC special assessment (p. 21).
- A Card net charge-off rate of about 3.6%.
- The outlook assumes the market-implied forward rate curve at June 30, 2026.

## Balance sheet and trends

Period-end loans were $761.4 billion (+1.3% sequentially, +4.3% year-on-year) and deposits were $1,031.6 billion (+0.7% sequentially). Total assets were $1,452.9 billion (pp. 5, 13). Over five quarters, net income rose from $6.12 billion to a peak of $6.56 billion in 1Q26 and then flattened. CET1 stayed at or above 14.9%, and headcount grew from 212,800 to 216,100 (p. 20).

## Inconsistencies and caveats in the source

- **ROTCE for 2Q26.**
  - The highlights state ROTCE of 25% (p. 3).
  - The non-GAAP reconciliation and the five-quarter table state 24%, down from 25% in 1Q26 (pp. 19, 20).
  - The reconciliation arithmetic supports 24%: $6,280 million of net income to common over $103,000 million of average tangible common equity, annualized, is about 24.4%.
  - Treat 24% as the supported figure and the headline as inconsistent.
- **Card net charge-off rate vs 1Q26.** The segment text compares 3.58% only with 2Q25 (p. 7), while the credit-quality table gives 3.55% for 1Q26 (p. 14).
- **Sensitivity vintage.** The NII sensitivity table is as of December 31, 2025, and the June 30, 2026 sensitivity is described only qualitatively, apart from the +100 bp figure (p. 15).
- **Preliminary and estimated items.** The SCB is preliminary, regulatory ratios are estimated pending the FR Y-9C, and the outlook and capital projections are forward-looking statements (pp. 3, 21).
- **Reported vs managed basis.** The managed revenue figure adds a $130 million fully-taxable-equivalent adjustment, giving $22,610 million (p. 19). Segment results are on a managed basis (p. 21), and the headline $22.48 billion is the reported figure.
