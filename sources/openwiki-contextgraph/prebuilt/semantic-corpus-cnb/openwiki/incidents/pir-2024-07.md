## Summary

PIR-2024-07 is the Digital Treasury Channels post-implementation review (PIR)
that closed out the **Wire Status Lite** pilot (tracked as
[CBO-3120](../projects/cbo-3120-wire-status-lite-pilot.md)) and the production
incident it caused, [INC-2024-1182](inc-2024-1182.md). Wire Status Lite piloted
auto-refreshing wire status in Crestline Business Online (CBO) for 60 clients
from January to May 2024: the browser polled the PRISM Payments Hub (PPH) v1
wire status API for every visible wire once every 30 seconds. On **2024-05-31**
(a month-end processing day), combined pilot polling and normal production
wire-release status refreshes drove roughly 85 TPS against the shared,
synchronous PPH v1 status API, exhausting its thread pool and delaying wire
release for 47 minutes. The pilot was terminated and the CBO-3120 epic was
closed as a direct outcome of this review.

Document identity and governance metadata (document ID, version, owner,
approvers, classification) are recorded separately in
[documents/pir-2024-07.md](../documents/pir-2024-07.md); this page carries the
full narrative content of the review: quantified impact, what the pilot
revealed about client understanding, root causes, and remediation actions —
including the one action still open.

## Impact

The PIR quantifies the business impact of the 2024-05-31 incident as follows
(quoted exactly from the review):

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Wires delayed | 1,240 |
| Wires released after the 6:00 p.m. ET Fedwire customer cutoff (value next day) | 312 |
| Clients compensated (interest claims) | 14 clients, $41,800 |
| Regulatory notification | Not required (no data exposure) |

No regulatory notification was required because the incident was a processing
delay, not a data exposure event. The 312 wires that missed the 6:00 p.m. ET
Fedwire customer cutoff had their value date pushed to the next business day,
which is what drove the interest-claim compensation paid to the 14 affected
clients.

## What the pilot revealed about clients

Beyond the operational outage, the PIR's user research during the pilot
surfaced a durable client-understanding problem with wire status labeling:

- **37% of surveyed pilot users believed "Processed" meant the beneficiary had
  received the funds.** In reality, the PPH v1 status API's `PROCESSED` value
  collapses several distinct internal lifecycle states — release, transmission
  to the network, network acknowledgment, and end-of-day accounting close —
  into one undifferentiated label (see
  [Settlement, Release, and "Completed" — Status Semantics](../concepts/settlement-release-completed-semantics.md)
  for the full breakdown of what `PROCESSED` actually spans). Clients had no
  way to distinguish a wire that had merely left CNB from one the beneficiary
  bank had actually acknowledged or settled.
- **What users valued most** was not granular lifecycle detail but three
  specific facts: confirmation that the wire had left CNB, the Fed reference
  (IMAD/OMAD) for their beneficiary, and when international wires were
  actually credited.
- **Held wires could not be explained.** Users asked Treasury Support why
  specific wires were held, and Treasury Support could not answer without
  Financial Crimes Compliance (FCC) guidance — the pilot exposed status
  information to clients without a corresponding, compliance-reviewed
  explanation path for holds.

This finding — that a client-facing status label was misread as a stronger
claim than it was designed to convey — is the same failure mode that later
recurred, a label generation later, when CBO's "Completed" relabeling
produced [CMP-2026-1189](../concepts/settlement-release-completed-semantics.md);
see the root-cause discussion below for why that recurrence is directly tied
to this PIR's one still-open action.

## Root causes

The PIR identifies three root causes for the incident and the client-facing
confusion it exposed:

1. **Polling architecture against a shared synchronous API with no
   client-side backoff.** Each of the 60 pilot clients' browsers polled the
   shared PPH v1 status API every 30 seconds for every visible wire, with no
   mechanism to back off under load; as latency increased, fixed-interval
   polling and retries added to the queue instead of relieving it.
2. **No capacity test at month-end volumes.** The pilot was never load-tested
   against the higher wire-release and status-refresh volumes characteristic
   of month-end processing days before being exposed to production traffic
   that stacked on the same thread pool.
3. **Status semantics were not validated with Compliance or Legal before
   exposure.** The "Processed" label was shown to clients without FCC or
   Legal review of what it would be reasonably understood to mean, which is
   the direct cause of the 37% client misunderstanding described above.

For the underlying mechanism by which the two load sources exhausted PPH's
thread pool and delayed wire release, see [INC-2024-1182](inc-2024-1182.md).

## Actions

| Action | Owner | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Rate-limit PPH v1 status API to 20 TPS shared | PPH | Done 2024-06 |
| Adopt event-driven status pattern (became ADR-PAY-019) | EA | Done 2024-06 |
| Build a reusable status projection service in CBO | CBO Platform | Delivered for ACH in 2025 (CBO-3815) |
| Validate client-facing status terminology with FCC and Legal before any future tracking feature | CBO Product | Open — carry forward |

Three of the four actions were completed within roughly a month of the
incident:

- The **20 TPS shared rate limit** on the PPH v1 status API was a direct,
  immediate guardrail against repeat thread-pool exhaustion, done in June
  2024.
- The **event-driven status pattern** action became
  [ADR-PAY-019](../decisions/adr-pay-019.md) (accepted 2024-06-11), which
  prohibits channel teams from polling PPH for status and instead requires
  consuming PPH lifecycle events into a channel-owned read model, limiting
  synchronous PPH status calls to throttled, user-initiated refresh.
- The **reusable status projection service** action was delivered as the CBO
  Status Projection Service, but only for ACH (CBO-3815, delivered 2025); as
  of later architecture reviews, Wire Center itself has not yet adopted this
  pattern and still relies on synchronous PPH v1 calls under ADR-PAY-019's
  throttled-refresh exception.

### Still-open action: validate status terminology with FCC and Legal

The fourth action — **validate client-facing status terminology with FCC and
Legal before any future tracking feature** — remains open, owned by CBO
Product, and is carried forward independently of ADR-PAY-019. It exists
specifically to close the gap the PIR identified: that a client-facing status
label ("Processed") was shipped without compliance or legal review of what
clients would understand it to mean, and that Treasury Support had no
FCC-approved way to explain held wires to clients.

This action was still open when CBO independently renamed "Processed" to
"Completed" in release R26.1 based on usability testing alone, without the
FCC/Legal terminology validation this PIR called for. That gap is the direct
cause of the later incident CMP-2026-1189, documented in
[Settlement, Release, and "Completed" — Status Semantics](../concepts/settlement-release-completed-semantics.md).
Any future wire (or other payment rail) status-tracking feature — including
further work on the CBO Status Projection Service pattern or an eventual
Wire Center migration to PPH v2 — must treat this action as a prerequisite
gate, not a parallel or retrospective step.
